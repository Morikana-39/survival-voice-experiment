# Methods wording for the implemented first version

This is a **prospective draft**. Change to past tense only after the study is actually conducted. Bracketed details require the researcher's confirmation; the website cannot establish them.

The study will use a single-factor, two-condition, between-subjects web-based experiment followed by a questionnaire. The experiment website is implemented using React and TypeScript with Vinext/Vite, a Cloudflare-compatible server runtime and SQLite/D1 storage, and is prepared for hosting through Sites. The first version is an owner-private preview; public recruitment has not begun.

Participants will be recruited through **[actual platforms used]**. Possible channels include WeChat, QQ and Xiaohongshu, subject to recruitment and ethics approval; these are examples, not a record of distribution. Each eligible adult will receive an individually issued, single-use participation code.

After informed consent and collection of age and gender, participants will be independently randomized with equal probability to fluent speech or speech containing filled pauses. The assignment and speech-fluency hypothesis will not be disclosed until debriefing. The assistant's voices will be generated using **[provider, model/version, voice identity and settings]** and recorded before data collection. Matched audio pairs will use identical substantive wording and voice settings, with only the planned filled pauses inserted in the disfluent condition. Audio quality and the manipulation will be checked before recruitment; recordings have not yet been supplied for this version.

Participants will be instructed to keep their device and browser sound on. A randomized tone-count task will check audibility before the main task. During each discussion, continuation will be gated on completion of the prerecorded response and confirmation that it was heard. Detectable player muting and playback interruptions will require replay. These procedures encourage audibility but **do not establish that hardware or browser-tab muting can always be detected**.

The decision task is adapted from the desert survival problem attributed to Lafferty and Eady (1974), as described in the supplied Nass et al. (1996) paper. Participants will imagine a mid-August crash landing at approximately 10 a.m. in the Sonoran Desert in the southwestern United States. Instructions and item descriptions will be in Simplified Chinese, with metric equivalents supplied. The current pilot contains **15 items** from the supplied list. If a 12-item version is intended, the exact exclusions must be determined before recruitment. The study does not currently claim direct consultation of the original 1974 materials.

Participants will first rank the items by importance to the group's survival and confirm their initial order. They will then discuss all items in the same predetermined order. An arrow in the ranking reference will identify the current item. For each item, participants will enter a brief reason, after which the assigned prerecorded assistant response will be played. Recordings will not respond dynamically to the submitted text.

Only after all discussions will participants see their initial ranking beside the assistant's ranking. The latter will be constructed entirely from their initial order using a fixed cyclic shift of three positions. This is a pilot operationalization inspired by the rank-displacement approach in Nass et al. (1996), not a claim to reproduce that paper's full mapping. Participants will then confirm a final ranking, whether changed or unchanged. All three rankings will be retained. The proposed conformity index is the reduction in Spearman footrule distance to the assistant's ranking, divided by initial distance. The primary metric and mapping must be finalized and preregistered before data collection.

Participants will next respond to five adapted social-presence items on a seven-point agreement scale, followed by debriefing. The items draw on Pitardi and Marriott (2021), Table 1. Their published table marks the personalness item as deleted; the current five-item questionnaire retains it to match the requested design. Therefore, describe this as a five-item adaptation of their listed items, not an unchanged validated five-item scale. Translation review and a pilot reliability assessment remain necessary.

Incomplete, withdrawn and preview records will be excluded from analysis, as will records without all required audio exposures. **[Any additional preregistered eligibility, attention or response-quality exclusions]** should be stated only after being decided. Database uniqueness constraints limit each participation code to one session, and completed sessions cannot be resubmitted. A secure session cookie permits recovery in the original browser. These measures limit repeat submissions, but preventing the same person from obtaining multiple codes depends on recruitment controls.

Before publication, complete the institution, researcher/contact, ethics approval, consent and debrief wording, retention/deletion period, actual recruitment dates/platforms, audio details, sample-size justification, final item count, preregistered scoring/exclusions, and actual hosting/access arrangements. Do not infer these from the preview implementation.
