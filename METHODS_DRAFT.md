# Methods wording for the implemented first version


Current revision (2026-09-08): eligibility is 12–65 inclusive. The new common warm-up, revised wording, read-only informational returns, swap interactions and preview reset are documented in `research/REVISION_2026-09-08.md`. This note supersedes earlier descriptions below of an unlimited upper age, heard-audio checkbox or absent warm-up; real collection remains disabled.

This is a **prospective draft**. Change to past tense only after the study is actually conducted. Bracketed details require the researcher's confirmation; the website cannot establish them.

The study will use a single-factor, two-condition, between-subjects web-based experiment followed by a questionnaire. The experiment website is implemented using React and TypeScript with Vinext/Vite, a Cloudflare-compatible server runtime and SQLite/D1 storage, and is prepared for hosting through Sites. The existing preview is publicly accessible for researcher testing; public recruitment has not begun.

Participants will be recruited through **[actual platforms used]**. Possible channels include WeChat, QQ and Xiaohongshu, subject to recruitment and ethics approval; these are examples, not a record of distribution. The proposed eligibility is age 12–65 inclusive. Each eligible participant will receive an individually issued, single-use participation code after the approved permission/consent process. Because adults are not excluded, the eventual sample should not automatically be described as exclusively teenage.

After the age-appropriate assent/consent and guardian-permission process required by the approved protocol, and collection of age and gender, participants will be independently randomized with equal probability to fluent speech or speech containing filled pauses. The assignment and speech-fluency hypothesis will not be disclosed until debriefing. The assistant's voices will be generated using **[provider, model/version, voice identity and settings]** and recorded before data collection. Matched audio pairs will use identical substantive wording and voice settings, with only the planned filled pauses inserted in the disfluent condition. Audio quality and the manipulation will be checked before recruitment. Twelve researcher-supplied fluent recordings are installed in the current preview; the matched disfluent recordings and provider details remain pending. Preview sessions audition fluent speech only and are excluded from the experiment dataset.

Participants will be instructed to keep their device and browser sound on. A randomized tone-count task will check audibility before the main task. During each discussion, continuation will be gated on completion of the prerecorded response. Detectable player muting and playback interruptions will require replay. These procedures encourage audibility but **do not establish that hardware or browser-tab muting can always be detected**.

The decision task is adapted from the desert survival problem attributed to Lafferty and Eady (1974), as described in the supplied Nass et al. (1996) paper. Participants will imagine a mid-August crash landing at approximately 10 a.m. in the Sonoran Desert in the southwestern United States. Instructions and item descriptions will be in Simplified Chinese, with metric equivalents supplied. The selected **12 items** are flashlight, jackknife, sectional air map, magnetic compass, gauze/compression kit, loaded .45-caliber pistol, red-and-white parachute, one US quart of water, Edible Animals of the Desert, sunglasses, top coat and cosmetic mirror. Plastic raincoat, salt tablets and vodka are excluded. This is a researcher-selected subset, not a claim to identify the precise subset used by Nass et al. The study does not currently claim direct consultation of the original 1974 materials.

Participants will first rank the items by importance to the group's survival and confirm their initial order. They will then discuss all items in the same predetermined order. An arrow in the ranking reference will identify the current item. For each item, participants will enter a brief reason, after which the assigned prerecorded assistant response will be played. Recordings will not respond dynamically to the submitted text.

Only after all 12 discussions will participants see their initial ranking beside the assistant's ranking. The latter will be constructed entirely from their initial order using the same fixed permutation for all participants: initial positions 1–12 map to AI positions [3, 6, 8, 1, 10, 4, 2, 11, 5, 12, 9, 7]. Every item changes position by 2–5 ranks. Initial Spearman footrule distance to the AI is consequently 42 for every participant, compared with a maximum possible distance of 72. This is a pilot operationalization inspired by the rank-displacement approach in Nass et al. (1996), not a claim to reproduce that paper's full mapping. Participants will then confirm a final ranking, whether changed or unchanged. All three rankings will be retained.

The proposed non-clipped conformity index is BCI = (42 − final footrule distance to AI)/42, with 1 denoting complete adoption, 0 no net reduction in disagreement, and negative values increased disagreement. Its range is −5/7 to 1. A bounded positive-conformity index, max(0, BCI), is additionally available on 0–1, but deliberately treats both increased disagreement and no net reduction as zero. Final alignment, 1 − final distance/72, is also saved as a distinct descriptive index; it is not the fraction of initial disagreement closed. These are operational adaptations, not formulas attributed to Nass. **[Preregister the primary metric and the role of secondary indices before recruitment, including whether clipping is appropriate.]** Do not select a metric after examining condition effects.

Participants will next respond to five adapted social-presence items on a seven-point agreement scale, followed by debriefing. The items draw on Pitardi and Marriott (2021), Table 1. Their published table marks the personalness item as deleted; the current five-item questionnaire retains it to match the requested design. Therefore, describe this as a five-item adaptation of their listed items, not an unchanged validated five-item scale. Translation review and a pilot reliability assessment remain necessary.

Incomplete, withdrawn and preview records will be excluded from analysis, as will records without all required audio exposures. **[Any additional preregistered eligibility, attention or response-quality exclusions]** should be stated only after being decided. Database uniqueness constraints limit each participation code to one session, and completed sessions cannot be resubmitted. A secure session cookie permits recovery in the original browser. These measures limit repeat submissions, but preventing the same person from obtaining multiple codes depends on recruitment controls.

Before publication, complete the institution, researcher/contact, ethics approval, consent and debrief wording, retention/deletion period, actual recruitment dates/platforms, audio details, sample-size justification, final item count, preregistered scoring/exclusions, and actual hosting/access arrangements. Do not infer these from the preview implementation.

The minor-participation process is not yet implemented or approved. Before inviting minors, obtain institutional ethics/privacy review of guardian permission, the young person's voluntary assent, any delayed disclosure, the sensitive-data provisions for children under 14, hosting/data location and deletion procedures. The current researcher preview should use fictional details only.
